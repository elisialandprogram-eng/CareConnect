You are working on the existing booking/appointment marketplace project.

This is an **implementation task, not an audit-only task**.

Your job is to inspect the existing implementation, identify every issue related to booking price derivation, tax calculation, tax configuration, pricing snapshots, and price display, and **fix the issues immediately when found**.

# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Establish one canonical pricing and tax system for the entire booking lifecycle.

The invariant must be:

```text
Service / Booking Inputs
        ↓
Canonical Revenue / Pricing Engine
        ↓
Canonical Tax Engine
        ↓
Immutable Booking Pricing Snapshot
        ↓
┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐
│ Patient UI   │ Provider UI  │ Email        │ Notification │
└──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘
                         ↓
                  Same exact numbers
```

There must be:

* One canonical price derivation.
* One canonical tax derivation.
* One canonical source of truth.
* No frontend tax calculations.
* No email-specific tax calculations.
* No notification-specific tax calculations.
* No provider-side recalculation that can produce a different result.
* No booking confirmation recalculation.
* No duplicated tax formulas in other routes.
* No conflicting legacy tax paths.

The quote calculation and final booking calculation may execute the canonical engine separately, but **they must receive the same authoritative inputs and produce the same result when nothing legitimately changed between quote and booking**.

Once a booking is created, its saved pricing snapshot is authoritative forever.

# 1. CANONICAL TAX MODEL

The tax system must have two clearly separated tax domains.

## A. SERVICE TAX

Service tax applies to the complete service-related taxable booking amount.

Service-related components include, where applicable:

* Base service price
* Home visit fee
* Clinic fee
* Telemedicine fee
* Emergency fee
* Surge fee
* Distance/travel fee
* Other service-related charges

These must be grouped into the service taxable bucket.

The service tax rate comes from:

```text
sub_service_tax_rules
```

using:

```text
sub-service + country + effective date + active status
```

The country-specific sub-service tax rule is the canonical source.

Do not use a single global:

```text
sub_services.tax_percentage
```

for new calculations.

---

# 2. PLATFORM TAX MODEL

Platform tax applies only to platform-related taxable components.

These can include:

* Platform fee
* Payment/admin charges
* Payment-method surcharge
* Other platform charges

Platform tax comes from the canonical platform tax configuration:

```text
tax_settings
```

using the authoritative booking/provider country and effective-date rules.

Do not mix service tax and platform tax into one rate.

---

# 3. IMPORTANT TAX BUSINESS RULE

Implement this exact business behavior:

### If the sub-service has a non-zero country-specific service tax:

That service tax applies to the entire service-related taxable booking amount.

Example:

```text
Base service          100
Home visit             20
Distance fee           10
Emergency fee           5
Surge                   5
-------------------------
Service taxable       140

Service tax = 27%

Service tax = 140 × 27%
            = 37.80
```

Platform charges are handled separately using platform tax.

### If the sub-service service-tax rate is 0%:

Service-related charges remain untaxed by the global/platform tax.

For example:

```text
Base service
Home visit
Distance fee
Emergency fee
Surge
```

must NOT suddenly receive the platform/global tax merely because service tax is zero.

Only platform-related charges such as:

```text
Platform fee
Payment/admin charges
```

are subject to platform tax.

This distinction must be enforced in the canonical tax engine.
